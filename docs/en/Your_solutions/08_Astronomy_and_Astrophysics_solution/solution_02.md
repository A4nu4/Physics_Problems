Calculate the orbital speed of the International Space Station (ISS), which orbits at an altitude of approximately 400 km above the Earth's surface. (Earth's mass $M_E \approx 5.97 \times 10^{24}$ kg). Compare this speed of Earth's orbital speed around the Sun (Earth-Sun distance $\approx 150 \times 10^6$ km, Earth's orbital period $\approx 365.25$ days). Which is faster, the ISS around the Earth or Earth in its orbit around the Sun?

---

To find out which is traveling faster, we will calculate the orbital speed of the International Space Station (ISS) around the Earth and compare it to the orbital speed of the Earth around the Sun.

---

### Part 1: Orbital Speed of the ISS ($v_{\text{ISS}}$)

An object in a stable, circular orbit requires a gravitational force that exactly balances the required centripetal force.

#### 1. Given Starting Values:

* **Mass of the Earth ($M_E$):** $5.97 \times 10^{24} \text{ kg}$
* **Radius of the Earth ($R_E$):** $\approx 6,371 \text{ km} = 6.371 \times 10^6 \text{ m}$
* **Altitude of the ISS ($h$):** $400 \text{ km} = 4.0 \times 10^5 \text{ m}$
* **Gravitational Constant ($G$):** $6.674 \times 10^{-11} \text{ N}\cdot\text{m}^2/\text{kg}^2$

The total orbital radius ($r$) is the distance from the center of the Earth to the ISS:


$$r = R_E + h = 6,371 \text{ km} + 400 \text{ km} = 6,771 \text{ km} = 6.771 \times 10^6 \text{ m}$$

#### 2. Calculation:

The formula for circular orbital velocity is:


$$v = \sqrt{\frac{G M_E}{r}}$$

Substitute the values:


$$v_{\text{ISS}} = \sqrt{\frac{(6.674 \times 10^{-11}) \times (5.97 \times 10^{24})}{6.771 \times 10^6}}$$

$$v_{\text{ISS}} = \sqrt{\frac{3.9844 \times 10^{14}}{6.771 \times 10^6}} = \sqrt{5.8845 \times 10^7}$$

$$v_{\text{ISS}} \approx 7,671 \text{ m/s} \approx \mathbf{7.67 \text{ km/s}}$$

---

### Part 2: Orbital Speed of the Earth around the Sun ($v_{\text{Earth}}$)

Since we are given the distance and the time it takes to complete one full revolution, we can find the speed by dividing the total circumference of Earth's orbit by its orbital period.

#### 1. Given Starting Values:

* **Earth-Sun Distance ($R$):** $150 \times 10^6 \text{ km} = 1.5 \times 10^{11} \text{ m}$
* **Orbital Period ($T$):** $365.25 \text{ days}$

Convert the period into seconds:


$$T = 365.25 \text{ days} \times 24 \text{ hours/day} \times 3600 \text{ seconds/hour} \approx 31,557,600 \text{ s}$$

#### 2. Calculation:

The linear speed along a circular path is:


$$v = \frac{2 \pi R}{T}$$

Substitute the values:


$$v_{\text{Earth}} = \frac{2 \times \pi \times (1.5 \times 10^{11} \text{ m})}{31,557,600 \text{ s}}$$

$$v_{\text{Earth}} = \frac{9.4248 \times 10^{11}}{31,557,600} \approx 29,865 \text{ m/s} \approx \mathbf{29.87 \text{ km/s}}$$

---

### Comparison and Final Conclusion

* **Orbital speed of the ISS around Earth:** $\approx 7.67 \text{ km/s}$ (about $27,600 \text{ km/h}$)
* **Orbital speed of Earth around the Sun:** $\approx 29.87 \text{ km/s}$ (about $107,500 \text{ km/h}$)

**The Earth traveling in its orbit around the Sun is much faster** than the ISS orbiting the Earth—nearly **4 times** faster!

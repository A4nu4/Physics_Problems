Calculate the linear speed (in km/s) of a point on the Earth's equator due to its rotation. Earth's radius $\approx 6378$ km.
---

To calculate the linear speed of a point on the Earth's equator due to its rotational velocity, we use the relationship between linear speed, radius, and the period of rotation.

---

### Given Starting Values:

* **Radius of the Earth at the Equator ($r$):** $\approx 6378\text{ km}$
* **Period of rotation ($T$):** The Earth completes one full rotation relative to the stars in a sidereal day, which is approximately $23\text{ hours, } 56\text{ minutes, and } 4\text{ seconds}$. For standard introductory physics calculations, we approximate this as exactly one solar day:

$$T = 24\text{ hours} = 24 \times 3600\text{ s} = 86,400\text{ s}$$



---

### 1. The Linear Speed Formula

A point on the equator travels a distance equal to the Earth's circumference ($2\pi r$) during one full rotational period ($T$). The linear speed ($v$) is given by:

$$v = \frac{2\pi r}{T}$$

---

### 2. Final Calculation

Substitute the values directly into the formula to find the speed in kilometers per second ($\text{km/s}$):

$$v = \frac{2 \cdot \pi \cdot 6378\text{ km}}{86,400\text{ s}}$$

$$v = \frac{40,074.16\text{ km}}{86,400\text{ s}}$$

$$v \approx 0.4638\text{ km/s}$$

---

### Final Result:

The linear speed of a point on the Earth's equator is approximately **$0.464\text{ km/s}$** (or about $1670\text{ km/h}$).

*(Note: If you use the highly precise sidereal day of $86,164\text{ s}$ for astronomical calculations, the speed is slightly higher at **$0.465\text{ km/s}$**).*

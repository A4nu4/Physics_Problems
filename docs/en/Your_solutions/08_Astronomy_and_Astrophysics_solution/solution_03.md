What is the acceleration due to gravity ($g$) at the altitude of the ISS (400 km)? Why do astronauts experience a state of "weightlessness" despite this gravity?
---

To find the acceleration due to gravity ($g$) at the altitude of the International Space Station (ISS), we use Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation.

---

### 1. Calculation of Gravity at 400 km Altitude

The acceleration due to gravity at any distance $r$ from the center of the Earth is given by the formula:

$$g = \frac{G M_E}{r^2}$$

#### Given Starting Values:

* **Gravitational Constant ($G$):** $6.674 \times 10^{-11} \text{ N}\cdot\text{m}^2/\text{kg}^2$
* **Mass of the Earth ($M_E$):** $5.972 \times 10^{24} \text{ kg}$
* **Mean Radius of the Earth ($R_E$):** $\approx 6371 \text{ km}$
* **Altitude of the ISS ($h$):** $400 \text{ km}$

The total distance ($r$) from the center of the Earth to the ISS is:


$$r = R_E + h = 6371 \text{ km} + 400 \text{ km} = 6771 \text{ km} = 6.771 \times 10^6 \text{ m}$$

#### Calculation:

$$g = \frac{(6.674 \times 10^{-11}) \cdot (5.972 \times 10^{24})}{(6.771 \times 10^6)^2}$$

$$g = \frac{3.9856 \times 10^{14}}{4.5846 \times 10^{13}} \approx 8.69 \text{ m/s}^2$$

At the altitude of the ISS, the acceleration due to gravity is approximately **$8.69 \text{ m/s}^2$**. This is about **89%** of the gravity we experience at sea level ($9.81 \text{ m/s}^2$). Earth's gravity up there is still very strong!

---

### 2. Why Do Astronauts Feel Weightless?

If gravity is almost 90% as strong as it is on the ground, why do astronauts float around as if there is no gravity at all?

The answer lies in the concept of **continuous free fall**.

* **The Cannonball Analogy:** Imagine standing on a giant mountain and firing a cannonball horizontally. Gravity pulls it down, and it hits the ground some distance away. If you fire it faster, it travels farther before landing.
* **Achieving Orbit:** If you fire the cannonball fast enough (about $7.67 \text{ km/s}$ for the ISS), the rate at which the cannonball falls toward the ground matches the exact rate at which the Earth's surface curves away beneath it.
* **Constant Falling:** The ISS, the astronauts, and everything inside are essentially dropping toward the center of the Earth, but because they are moving sideways so fast, they keep missing the planet.

Because both the spacecraft and the astronauts are falling together with the exact same gravitational acceleration ($8.69 \text{ m/s}^2$), there is no normal force (like a floor or a chair) pushing back up against the astronauts to give them the sensation of weight. This state is more accurately called **microgravity** or **apparent weightlessness**.

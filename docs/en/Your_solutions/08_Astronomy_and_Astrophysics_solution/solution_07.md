A "Dyson Sphere" is a hypothetical megastructure that completely encompasses a star to capture its energy output. If the mass of Mercury ($3.3 \times 10^{23}$ kg) were used to build a solar panel sphere with a surface density of 10 kg/m², what would be the radius of the sphere?
---

To find the radius of the hypothetical Dyson Sphere, we need to connect the total available mass, the required surface density, and the geometry of a sphere.

### 1. Mathematical Formulas

The mass ($M$) of a hollow sphere with a uniform surface density ($\sigma$) is equal to its total surface area ($A$) multiplied by that density:

$$M = \sigma \cdot A$$

The surface area of a sphere with radius $R$ is given by:

$$A = 4\pi R^2$$

Substituting the area formula into the mass equation gives:

$$M = \sigma \cdot (4\pi R^2)$$

---

### 2. Solve for the Radius ($R$)

Rearranging the formula to isolate $R$:

$$R^2 = \frac{M}{4\pi \sigma}$$

$$R = \sqrt{\frac{M}{4\pi \sigma}}$$

**Given Values:**

* **Mass of Mercury ($M$):** $3.3 \times 10^{23} \text{ kg}$
* **Surface Density ($\sigma$):** $10 \text{ kg/m}^2$

---

### 3. Final Calculation

Substitute the numbers into our equation:

$$R = \sqrt{\frac{3.3 \times 10^{23} \text{ kg}}{4\pi \cdot 10 \text{ kg/m}^2}}$$

$$R = \sqrt{\frac{3.3 \times 10^{23}}{125.6637}}$$

$$R = \sqrt{2.626 \times 10^{21}} \approx 5.12 \times 10^{10} \text{ m}$$

---

### Final Result:

The radius of the Dyson Sphere would be approximately:

* **$5.12 \times 10^{10} \text{ meters}$**
* **$51.2 \times 10^6 \text{ kilometers}$** * **$0.34 \text{ AU}$** *(Astronomical Units, where $1 \text{ AU}$ is the Earth-Sun distance)*

### Physical Context:

For perspective, Mercury's actual average orbital distance from the Sun is about **$57.9 \times 10^6 \text{ km}$** (or $0.39 \text{ AU}$). This means if we completely disassembled the planet Mercury to build a shell with this thickness, the resulting Dyson Sphere would sit just **inside** Mercury's original orbit!

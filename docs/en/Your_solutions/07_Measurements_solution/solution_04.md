A car's speedometer has a 5% of uncertainty. If it reads 60 km/h, what is the range of the car's actual speed?

---

To find the range of the car's actual speed, we need to calculate the absolute uncertainty based on the given percentage and apply it to the speedometer reading.

### 1. Calculate the Absolute Uncertainty

The speedometer has a **5%** relative uncertainty on a reading of **60 km/h**.

$$\text{Absolute Uncertainty} = 60\text{ km/h} \times 5\%$$

$$\text{Absolute Uncertainty} = 60 \times 0.05 = 3\text{ km/h}$$

This means the reading is written as $(60 \pm 3)\text{ km/h}$.

---

### 2. Determine the Speed Range

To find the possible interval of the car's actual speed, we subtract and add the absolute uncertainty from the reading:

* **Minimum possible speed:** $60 - 3 = 57\text{ km/h}$
* **Maximum possible speed:** $60 + 3 = 63\text{ km/h}$

---

### Final Result:

The range of the car's actual speed is from **57 km/h to 63 km/h**.
